# ATLAS Teasers — Gradual Pre-Launch Promotion

## Overall intent

The creator may introduce ATLAS gradually before the dedicated Building ATLAS series begins.
The aim is to build a little familiarity and curiosity without revealing the full plan.
Teasers should support regular BitBlock videos, not replace their main subject.
The creator wants control over how much of ATLAS is shown.
Do not treat promotional ideas as commitments until the creator confirms them.

## Embedded teaser segments

The creator may include brief ATLAS teaser segments inside ordinary BitBlock videos.
The approximate duration discussed was **30–40 seconds**.
The segment should show a small glimpse of ATLAS and its main idea.
It should reveal only a limited amount of what has already been planned.
The purpose is to create curiosity and make the ATLAS name familiar.
It is not intended to explain the full product or every planned feature.
It is not intended to walk through the architecture in detail.
The creator does not currently intend to make dedicated long-form explanation videos for this early hype phase.
These short glimpses are a lightweight way to foreshadow the project.
Treat the duration as a rough guide, not an exact rule.

## What a glimpse may communicate

A teaser can signal that a larger project is being planned.
It can hint at the problem ATLAS aims to address.
It can show one carefully chosen visual or small part of the concept.
It can tell viewers that more of the journey may be shared later.
It should avoid implying that planned features are already implemented.
It should not reveal details the creator wants to keep private.
It should make sense to a viewer who has never heard of ATLAS.
It should fit naturally into the regular video's pacing.
The regular video's topic must remain the primary reason to watch.

## Dedicated teaser Shorts — later possibility

The creator also raised the possibility of standalone ATLAS teaser Shorts.
These would be purpose-made short-form videos about ATLAS.
They are not planned from the beginning of the channel.
The creator may consider them after BitBlock has received some views.
This idea is conditional and optional.
No view threshold has been chosen.
No subscriber threshold has been chosen.
No number of Shorts has been chosen.
No publication date or cadence has been chosen.
Do not schedule or start a Shorts campaign automatically.
Revisit the idea only when the channel has some audience response and the creator wants to proceed.

## Distinguish the formats

An embedded teaser is a brief segment inside a regular BitBlock upload.
A dedicated teaser Short is a separate upload made specifically for ATLAS.
The first may be used earlier as a subtle preview.
The second is a possible later step after the channel has gained some views.
Do not conflate the 30–40-second segment with a commitment to publish Shorts.
A teaser segment does not require a separate upload.
A dedicated Short should have its own clear teaser purpose if the creator chooses to make one.

## Main-agent checklist

Keep teaser suggestions small, selective, and creator-controlled.
Do not invent unrevealed product features for teaser copy.
Do not describe planned features as working software.
Do not reveal more than the creator has approved.
Do not begin dedicated Shorts at channel launch based only on this idea.
Ask before turning optional Shorts into an actual content plan.
Make sure teaser material does not distract from the regular video's value.

## Additional operating notes
- Treat every teaser as optional and subject to creator approval.
- Keep embedded teasers and standalone Shorts explicitly distinct.

# Initial Video Formats — Before Building ATLAS

## Purpose of the early phase

Before Building ATLAS begins, the creator expects to publish regular BitBlock videos.
These videos will use the creator's current setup and available tools.
The aim is to establish the channel through useful, interesting standalone content.
ATLAS is not intended to be the channel's launch content.
The exact first-video list has not been decided.
Do not turn examples in this document into a fixed upload queue.
The early phase should leave room for new interests and discoveries.

## Three intended approaches

The creator explicitly wants to use all three approaches below.
They are complementary formats, not competing channel strategies.
A single video can combine more than one approach.
The channel should not be labelled tutorial-only because tutorials are one part of the mix.

### Beginner tutorials

Beginner tutorials explain a tool, concept, or workflow accessibly.
They should help viewers understand what something is and how to get started.
A tutorial can show setup, first use, common options, or a simple useful outcome.
Explain assumptions and prerequisites instead of expecting prior knowledge.
Use clear steps, but avoid padding the video with unnecessary detail.
Verify commands, versions, and settings before presenting them as reliable.
Distinguish a personal preferred setup from the only correct way.
A beginner-friendly explanation can still be technically accurate and thoughtful.

### Documented experiments

Experiments begin with a question, curiosity, or idea worth testing.
Show what is being attempted and why it is interesting.
Explain the setup enough for viewers to understand the test.
Capture meaningful stages rather than every trivial action.
Share the actual result, including surprises, limitations, or failure.
An experiment does not need to succeed to become a useful video.
Do not rewrite an unsuccessful result as a success story.
Explain what was learned and what might be tried next.
Keep claims proportional to what the experiment demonstrates.

### Interesting workflows

Workflow videos show a useful or interesting way to accomplish something.
They may combine tools, settings, scripts, or repeatable steps.
Explain the problem the workflow solves before showing details.
Show the outcome so viewers can judge whether it is useful.
Mention trade-offs, limitations, and situations where it may not fit.
A workflow can be practical, exploratory, or an interesting discovery.
Avoid claiming that a personal workflow is universally optimal.

## Current setup examples

WSL was specifically mentioned as an example of a tool for early videos.
Docker Desktop was also specifically mentioned.
These are examples of the creator's current setup, not a complete topic list.
A video could teach a beginner concept involving one of these tools.
A video could document an experiment using one of these tools.
A video could demonstrate a workflow combining tools.
Do not assume every early video must involve both WSL and Docker Desktop.
Do not assume a particular tutorial or experiment has already been selected.

## Combining formats

A beginner tutorial may include a small experiment to demonstrate a feature.
An experiment may pause to explain a beginner concept.
A workflow video may teach each component as it is introduced.
Choose the format based on the viewer's main reason to watch.
Use other approaches as supporting elements when they improve clarity.
Do not force every video to contain all three approaches.

## Main-agent checklist

Offer a varied shortlist across all three approaches when asked to brainstorm.
Mark ideas as possibilities, not approved uploads.
Keep early suggestions compatible with the creator's current setup where practical.
Do not assume every video must promote ATLAS.
Do not impose a fixed order, cadence, or topic quota.
Preserve room for the creator to choose based on interest and available time.

## 1. Tech should clearly outweigh Gaming

**Creator clarification — confirmed direction**

BitBlock is tech-first. Tech content should be published more often and receive more attention than Gaming. Gaming is a smaller side interest / side project within the channel, not an equally weighted pillar.

Implications for future planning:
- Do not assume an even Tech-to-Gaming upload split.
- Do not manufacture Gaming uploads simply to balance the channel.
- Let the creator's available time and genuine interest determine when a Gaming video is made.
- Keep Tech as the dominant part of the channel's output and identity.

No exact percentage or upload ratio has been chosen.

## 2. The initial pre-ATLAS video mix

**Creator clarification — confirmed direction; exact topics remain undecided**

Before the Building ATLAS series begins, the creator expects to make videos using the current setup, including tools such as WSL and Docker Desktop. The exact first-video list has not been finalised.

The intended mix includes all three of these approaches:

1. **Beginner tutorials** — accessible, practical explanations that help viewers get started with a tool, concept, or workflow.
2. **Documented experiments** — trying something, showing the process and outcome, and sharing what was learned. Experiments do not need to succeed to make a worthwhile video.
3. **Interesting workflows** — demonstrating useful, unusual, or satisfying ways of combining tools and getting things done.

These are complementary formats, not mutually exclusive categories. A single video may combine a tutorial, an experiment, and a workflow demonstration.

Examples of the current-tooling territory mentioned in the conversation include WSL and Docker Desktop. These are examples, not a locked list of launch videos.

## 3. Gaming's likely on-channel form

**Creator clarification — examples of intended content**

When the creator does make Gaming videos, Minecraft is the main likely focus. The styles mentioned were:
- ordinary Survival gameplay;
- Hardcore gameplay;
- building projects and showing builds;
- custom modded Minecraft gameplay.

Other games may appear occasionally, but Minecraft is the expected centre of the Gaming side.

This should be interpreted alongside the Tech-first balance above: these are possible Gaming formats, not a promise of frequent Gaming uploads or a fixed series schedule.

## 4. Tentative ATLAS planning and development window

**Creator estimate — tentative, not a deadline**

The current rough target discussed was:

- **February 2027:** begin ATLAS planning and design episodes.
- **Mid-to-late March 2027:** potentially begin actual ATLAS implementation and development-session episodes.

This is a refinement of the earlier broad idea of starting ATLAS around February 2027: February is now more specifically associated with the planning phase, while implementation may begin around the middle or end of March.

The dates are estimates and may move. Do not present them publicly as guaranteed release dates or treat them as a fixed production calendar.

Until the ATLAS planning phase begins, the creator expects BitBlock to focus on standalone Tech content and occasional Gaming content.

## 5. Early ATLAS teasers inside regular videos

**Creator idea — likely approach, details flexible**

Before the dedicated Building ATLAS series begins, the creator may start introducing ATLAS through brief teaser segments embedded in ordinary BitBlock videos.

The initial teaser concept:
- Use roughly **30–40 seconds** within a regular video.
- Give viewers a small glimpse of ATLAS and the main idea.
- Reveal only a limited amount of what has been planned.
- Aim to build curiosity and familiarity with the ATLAS name.
- Do not turn these early segments into full explanations of the product, its architecture, or every planned feature.

The creator does not currently intend to make dedicated long-form ATLAS explanation/introduction videos just for this early hype phase. These short in-video glimpses are a lightweight way to foreshadow the project while the channel's regular content continues.

Treat the duration as an approximate creative guideline, not a strict rule for every appearance.

## 6. Possible dedicated ATLAS teaser Shorts — later, conditional

**Creator idea — explicitly tentative and delayed**

The creator may eventually make standalone short-form videos / YouTube Shorts specifically tailored as ATLAS teasers.

Important timing and status:
- Not from the beginning of the channel.
- Consider only after BitBlock has received some views and begun building an audience.
- These would be purpose-made ATLAS teaser Shorts, distinct from the 30–40-second teaser segments embedded in regular videos.
- The creator has not specified a view threshold, launch date, number of Shorts, or a commitment to make them.

Do not schedule or begin a Shorts campaign automatically. Keep it as a possible later promotional idea, to revisit when there is some audience response and the creator wants to proceed.

## 7. Suggested sequencing of the pre-ATLAS build-up

This is a synthesis of the creator's stated direction, not a fixed calendar:

1. Establish BitBlock through regular, mostly Tech videos using the current setup. Mix beginner tutorials, documented experiments, and interesting workflows. Publish Gaming less frequently as a side project.
2. Once it feels appropriate, begin placing occasional 30–40-second ATLAS glimpses inside relevant regular videos. Keep the reveals small and intriguing.
3. If the channel has gained some views and an initial audience, consider whether dedicated ATLAS teaser Shorts would be worthwhile. This step is optional.
4. Around February 2027, tentatively begin the Building ATLAS planning/design phase.
5. Around mid-to-late March 2027, tentatively begin recording and documenting actual development sessions.

The creator may change this order or timing. Do not let promotional activity distract from making useful regular videos or from the actual ATLAS project.

# 2026 Milestone Research Log

**Purpose:** Maintain an evidence-based record of real 2026 achievements for the album's lyrics.  
**Research cutoff for this pass:** 25 November 2026.  
**Scope note:** This is a living collection. Entries are candidates, not automatic lyric approvals.

## Research rules

A claim is eligible for lyrics only after its source and status are checked. Prefer primary sources:
- research papers and official journals;
- space agencies and mission teams;
- universities and research laboratories;
- official company or project announcements for product releases;
- government and intergovernmental organisations;
- reputable independent reporting for context and corroboration.

For every candidate, record:
1. **Milestone:** What happened, in precise language.
2. **Date:** When it happened or was published.
3. **Organisation / people:** Who conducted or announced it.
4. **Field / track:** Where it belongs.
5. **Status:** Demonstrated result, paper/publication, announcement, release, ongoing programme, or future plan.
6. **Evidence:** Direct source link(s).
7. **Caveat:** Limits, uncertainty, or context needed to avoid exaggeration.
8. **Lyric use:** Proposed accurate phrasing, if approved.

## Important distinctions

- A paper published in 2026 is not automatically a discovery made in 2026.
- An announcement is not proof that a technology is fully deployed.
- A prototype demonstration is not the same as a mature commercial product.
- A mission launch, flyby, landing, observation, and scientific result are different milestones.
- A planned event or forecast must not be written as if it has already happened.
- Avoid unsupported “first ever”, “world's best”, and superlative claims.
- Company names may be included where relevant, but the song should focus on what was achieved and why it matters.
- Company-reported benchmark scores must be labelled as company-reported unless independently replicated.
- Benchmark performance is not the same as real-world reliability, scientific discovery, or deployment success.

## Track 02 — THE AGE OF INTELLIGENCE

### A. Research systems and AI-assisted discovery

#### 1. Empirical Research Assistance (ERA)
- **Date:** 19 May 2026.
- **Organisation:** Research team publishing in *Nature*.
- **Status:** Peer-reviewed research publication describing an AI system.
- **What happened:** ERA combines a large language model with tree search to create and improve scientific software against measurable quality criteria.
- **Reported results:** In bioinformatics, the authors report 40 new methods for single-cell data analysis that outperformed the leading human-developed methods on a public leaderboard. In epidemiology, ERA generated 14 models that outperformed the CDC ensemble and other individual models for forecasting COVID-19 hospitalisations.
- **Why it matters:** AI can contribute to the construction and optimisation of research tools, not merely summarise papers or answer questions.
- **Caveat:** These are task- and benchmark-specific results. They do not mean AI independently conducted and experimentally verified 40 new biological discoveries or replaced scientists.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “From data into methods, new paths for science to explore.”
- **Sources:** [Nature paper — An AI system to help scientists write expert-level empirical software](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10658-6) · [Google Research — Science AI overview](https://research.google/research-areas/science-ai/)

#### 2. Co-Scientist
- **Date:** 19 May 2026.
- **Organisation:** Google Research and collaborators; paper published in *Nature*.
- **Status:** Peer-reviewed research publication describing a multi-agent research system.
- **What happened:** Co-Scientist is built on Gemini and uses agents that generate, critique and refine scientific hypotheses in response to research goals and prior evidence.
- **Why it matters:** It demonstrates a structured way to use multiple AI agents to help researchers explore hypotheses and decide what might merit experimental testing.
- **Caveat:** A generated hypothesis is not a confirmed discovery. Experimental validation remains essential.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “Many minds of silicon, proposing what to test.”
- **Source:** [Nature paper — Accelerating scientific discovery with Co-Scientist](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10644-y)

#### 3. Paper2Agent
- **Date:** 16 September 2026.
- **Organisation:** Academic research team; paper published in *Nature*.
- **Status:** Peer-reviewed research publication describing a framework.
- **What happened:** Paper2Agent converts research papers into interactive AI agents, aiming to make the methods and findings in papers more usable and explorable.
- **Why it matters:** It points toward research outputs becoming interactive tools rather than remaining static documents.
- **Caveat:** This is a framework for interacting with published research, not evidence that an AI independently validates every paper or makes new discoveries.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “The pages become pathways, knowledge made alive.”
- **Source:** [Nature paper — Reimagining research papers as interactive and reliable AI agents](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11044-y)

#### 4. MedChemBench
- **Date:** 20 November 2026.
- **Organisation:** OpenAI.
- **Status:** Public benchmark and accompanying research release.
- **What happened:** MedChemBench evaluates whether AI models can reconstruct molecular structures, interpret experimental evidence, compare compounds, select candidates and predict properties. It contains 99 problems drawn from 117 papers and patents, covering 761 distinct molecular structures.
- **Reported result:** OpenAI reports that its GPT-6.2 Astra model earned 84.5% of available credit at the xhigh reasoning setting in the benchmark's headline comparison. The release also reports that compound selection and numerical property prediction remained substantially harder than structure reconstruction and scientific explanation.
- **Why it matters:** It tests a more specialised form of scientific reasoning: connecting molecular structure with experimental evidence and trade-offs.
- **Caveat:** OpenAI created and reported the benchmark. The company explicitly says the results do not establish that models can discover new drugs or improve laboratory decisions; prior exposure to source material cannot be ruled out, and the evaluation used single attempts without tools, feedback or new experiments.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “Molecules mapped through evidence, but the lab must prove the answer.”
- **Source:** [OpenAI — Can AI help scientists identify promising drug candidates?](https://openai.com/index/introducing-medchembench/)

### B. Frontier model releases and capability competition

These are genuine 2026 product/research announcements, but claims about being “best”, “frontier”, or achieving a benchmark record must remain attributed to the releasing organisation unless independently confirmed.

#### 5. Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2
- **Date:** 9 November 2026.
- **Organisation:** Anthropic.
- **Status:** Company-announced model releases.
- **What was announced:** New models positioned for demanding technical work, coding, finance, science and agentic tasks. Anthropic also described work in scientific research and cybersecurity.
- **Caveat:** Product descriptions, internal evaluations and customer testimonials are not independent proof of general capability. Any specific scientific result should be checked against the relevant preprint, paper or external validation before lyric use.
- **Possible lyric angle:** Use as part of the broader global model-development race, not as an unsupported “world's best” claim.
- **Source:** [Anthropic — Introducing Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)

#### 6. GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol
- **Date:** 17 November 2026.
- **Organisation:** OpenAI.
- **Status:** Company-announced model releases.
- **What was announced:** Astra was positioned for demanding professional and scientific work; Sol for more efficient everyday work. The announcement describes improvements in coding, computer use, agentic workflows, scientific evaluations and efficiency.
- **Additional safety context:** OpenAI said Astra met its “High” threshold for AI self-improvement capability under its Preparedness Framework and described additional monitoring and safeguards.
- **Caveat:** The performance comparisons and capability classifications are OpenAI-reported. The self-improvement classification is a company risk assessment, not a claim that the model autonomously builds a successor.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “New engines of reasoning, more capable with every turn.”
- **Source:** [OpenAI — Introducing GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol/)

#### 7. Gemini 4 Neon
- **Date:** 18 November 2026; page updated 23 November 2026.
- **Organisation:** Google DeepMind.
- **Status:** Company-announced model release, available through Google's products and API.
- **What was announced:** A model designed for complex software engineering, knowledge work and science workflows, with a one-million-token context window and support for processing varied enterprise inputs. Google reported results on external benchmarks including agent and scientific-reasoning evaluations.
- **Caveat:** Google's benchmark claims should be checked against the linked benchmark methodologies and independent evaluations. A long context window does not guarantee perfect recall or reasoning.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “Across a million tokens, new constellations of context.”
- **Source:** [Google — Introducing Gemini 4 Neon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-neon/)

#### 8. Genie 4
- **Date:** 27 October 2026.
- **Organisation:** Google DeepMind.
- **Status:** Research announcement / limited research preview.
- **What happened:** Google DeepMind announced Genie 4, a multimodal world model designed to create interactive simulated environments with coherent visual scenes, sound and interaction.
- **Why it matters:** It represents progress toward systems that model and generate environments people can explore and influence, rather than producing only static text or images.
- **Caveat:** A generated simulation is not itself proof of a complete, accurate physical-world model. The announcement described a research preview, not unrestricted general availability.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “Worlds that answer, landscapes shaped by thought.”
- **Source:** [Google DeepMind — Genie 4: A new generation of world simulation](https://deepmind.google/blog/genie-4-a-new-generation-of-world-simulation/)

### C. Research access and the wider ecosystem

#### 9. ChatGPT for Academic Researchers expansion
- **Date:** 16 November 2026.
- **Organisation:** OpenAI.
- **Status:** Programme expansion announcement.
- **What happened:** OpenAI announced an expansion intended to provide up to 200,000 researchers worldwide with access to ChatGPT, including frontier models, through an academic programme.
- **Why it matters:** The story is not only about building more capable models; it is also about making AI tools available to research teams.
- **Caveat:** This is a programme target and access initiative, not evidence that 200,000 researchers had already received access or made discoveries with the tools.
- **Possible lyric angle:** “More hands reach for the questions once beyond their reach.”
- **Source:** [OpenAI — Broadening access to AI for scientists](https://openai.com/index/expanding-chatgpt-for-academic-researchers/)

## D. Important context — not a 2026 milestone list

### Stanford 2026 AI Index
The 2026 AI Index is useful for broad context on capability trends, benchmarks, AI adoption, science and responsible AI. However, many of its technical performance charts describe results through 2025 or March 2026. Do not turn a retrospective statistic into a claim that the underlying breakthrough happened in 2026.

- **Use:** Background and context for the song's themes.
- **Do not use:** As proof that every reported capability was newly achieved during 2026.
- **Source:** [Stanford HAI — 2026 AI Index Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)

## E. Claims requiring extra caution

### OpenAI / Navier–Stokes claim
- **Date:** 8 September 2026.
- **Status:** Company claim reported by *Nature* news; not approved as a confirmed mathematical solution in this log.
- **What was claimed:** OpenAI said an AI system had generated a solution related to the Navier–Stokes Millennium Problem.
- **Caveat:** A claim about a major open mathematical problem requires independent expert scrutiny and formal validation. Do not write “AI solved the Navier–Stokes problem” as an established fact unless that status is independently confirmed.
- **Source:** [Nature — OpenAI claims huge maths breakthrough on a famed ‘Millennium Problem’](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02842-5)

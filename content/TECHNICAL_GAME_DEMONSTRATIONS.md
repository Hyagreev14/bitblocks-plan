# Technical Game Demonstrations

## Purpose
This document defines the limited circumstances in which game footage can appear on BitBlock. BitBlock is a tech-only channel and does not publish gaming-focused videos.

## Allowed use
Game footage may be included when it directly helps explain or prove a technical point. Examples include:
- Showing a Minecraft mod feature working in-game during a mod-development video.
- Demonstrating the result of a technical change, configuration, or compatibility test.
- Showing a reproducible technical behaviour that would be difficult to explain with screenshots or diagrams alone.

## The video must remain technical
The main subject and viewer promise must be about development, code, tooling, configuration, debugging, testing, performance, compatibility, or another technical topic. The game is the demonstration environment, not the subject of the video.

For example, a video explaining how a custom mod feature was designed and implemented may include a short in-game demonstration of that feature. A video whose main purpose is to play Minecraft, explore a world, complete a challenge, or showcase ordinary gameplay is not suitable for BitBlock.

## Not part of the channel
Do not plan or publish:
- Let's Plays or ordinary gameplay episodes.
- Survival or Hardcore series.
- Gaming challenges or gameplay experiments whose main focus is playing.
- Ordinary Minecraft building or world tours.
- Game reviews or gaming commentary as standalone entertainment content.
- Gameplay footage added only as decoration when it does not support the technical explanation.

## Presentation guidance
- Introduce the technical question or feature before showing the game footage.
- Capture only the footage needed to demonstrate the relevant behaviour.
- Use narration, labels, or callouts to connect the footage to the technical explanation.
- Return to code, configuration, tooling, or test results where useful.
- Verify the game version, mod loader, mod version, and relevant compatibility claims.
- Distinguish what was personally tested from what is generally supported.

## Simple test
Ask: **If the gameplay footage were removed, would the video's main technical explanation still be the reason to watch?** If yes, the footage is likely supporting a technical demonstration. If the main appeal disappears because viewers were primarily watching someone play, it is a gaming video and does not fit BitBlock.

# Editorial Principles

## 1. Be honest
Never claim a test succeeded if it was not run. Never invent benchmarks, compatibility, performance gains, quotes, or project progress. Explain uncertainty.

## 2. Explain the why
A useful video goes beyond showing clicks or commands. Explain the problem, the chosen approach, the trade-offs, and what the result means.

## 3. Show real work
Bugs, failed attempts, revisions, and changed plans can be valuable parts of a development story. Do not manufacture drama or hide every imperfection.

## 4. Keep the tone natural
The voice should be curious, clear, relaxed, and confident without sounding like a corporate presentation. Avoid forced hype, exaggerated claims, and repetitive calls to action.

## 5. Respect the viewer's time
Start with the premise and value. Remove long dead sections, repeated explanations, and irrelevant setup. A video can be long when the subject deserves it.

## 6. Keep technical claims grounded
For software and hardware topics, verify versions, commands, compatibility, security implications, and current behaviour before publication. State the environment used for testing.

## 7. Do not over-script spontaneity
Scripts are scaffolding. The creator can change wording, order, or format while recording. A natural explanation is better than reading a rigid document.

## 8. Keep safety and privacy in scope
Do not expose credentials, private account data, personal messages, school information, or identifying details in captures. Explain cybersecurity defensively; do not turn a demonstration into instructions for harming systems.

## 9. Make the result understandable
Show what changed, how to recognise success, and any limitations or next steps.

## 10. The creator decides
AI agents may propose, draft, critique, and research. The creator approves the direction, claims, tone, and final upload.

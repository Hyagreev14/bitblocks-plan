# Episode Planning

## One-page episode brief
For each substantial video, record:
- **Working title:** a clear description of the technical topic.
- **Format:** beginner tutorial, explainer, documented experiment, interesting workflow, planning, development timelapse, review, or other.
- **Viewer promise:** what will the viewer understand or be able to do?
- **Premise:** what question, problem, or story drives the video?
- **Environment:** versions, hardware, software, and relevant test context.
- **Evidence:** what must be tested, captured, or researched?
- **Visuals:** screen captures, timelapse, diagrams, code, mockups, or technical demonstration footage.
- **Narration:** full script or outline.
- **Risks:** privacy, technical uncertainty, scope, and production constraints.
- **Ending:** result, limitations, and natural next step.

If game footage is proposed, explain which technical behaviour it demonstrates. It must support the technical story rather than turn the video into gameplay content.

## Scope
An episode should have one main story. If the outline contains several unrelated topics, split it or narrow the focus. If the idea is too small, combine it with a related update rather than padding it.

## ATLAS-specific planning
For a Building ATLAS episode, identify the planning topic, session, or milestone; list the meaningful changes; note any decisions worth explaining; and confirm that the capture sequence is usable. Planning episodes should distinguish proposals from implementation. Development-episode voiceover should explain the work, not merely describe the screen.

## Status labels
Use simple labels such as idea, researching, planned, captured, editing, ready, published. Do not mark a video as captured or published until it actually is.

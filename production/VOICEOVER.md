# Voiceover Guide

## Role of narration
The voiceover is the explanation layer for the video. In the ATLAS session series, every episode should have narration explaining the work done in that session. The timelapse alone is not expected to communicate the story.

## A useful outline
- **Opening:** identify the session's goal and why it matters.
- **Context:** briefly explain what already exists or what led to this task.
- **Work:** explain the main changes and the reasoning behind them.
- **Challenges:** describe important bugs, uncertainties, or trade-offs.
- **Result:** say what works, what is incomplete, and how it was checked.
- **Next:** identify the natural next step without promising a fixed release date.

## Narration style
Speak as if explaining the work to a curious person. Define unfamiliar terms when useful. Avoid reading every line of code, narrating every mouse movement, or filling silence with unnecessary words.

## Script versus outline
Use a full script for complex technical explanations, precise claims, or videos where wording matters. Use an outline for familiar work where a natural explanation will sound better. It is fine to combine both.

## Recording
- Record a short test and listen for clipping, noise, and volume.
- Keep a consistent distance from the microphone.
- Speak clearly and leave small pauses between ideas.
- Re-record sections that are difficult to understand.
- Keep a backup of the clean voice track until the edit is final.

## Editing
Remove long pauses and repeated phrases, but preserve a natural cadence. Keep music below speech and avoid sound effects that distract from technical explanation.

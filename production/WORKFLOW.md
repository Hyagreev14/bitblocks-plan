# Production Workflow

## 1. Select the idea
Write one sentence describing the viewer promise. BitBlock is a technology channel, so the idea should be about a tool, concept, workflow, experiment, or technical project. Choose a format that suits the story: tutorial, explainer, documented experiment, workflow demonstration, development episode, planning episode, or review.

Game footage may be included only when it directly demonstrates a technical subject. It must not change the video into gameplay-focused content.

## 2. Check feasibility
Estimate the work needed for research, capture, narration, and editing. Confirm that the idea is worth the effort. Reduce scope rather than forcing an unrealistic schedule. A video should not be made solely to fill an upload slot.

## 3. Research and test
For technical topics, verify current versions, commands, compatibility, and relevant risks. Test the exact workflow where possible. Record the environment, assumptions, limitations, and sources. Separate tested results from expectations and opinions.

## 4. Prepare visuals
Collect only the material needed: screenshots, diagrams, timelapse images, mockups, code examples, or technical demonstration footage. Ensure that visuals support the narration. For ATLAS planning episodes, diagrams and design notes may be central. For ATLAS development episodes, use the screenshot timelapse workflow. If game footage is used, capture only what is needed to demonstrate the technical result.

## 5. Prepare narration
Identify the beginning, main explanation, and outcome. Use a full script when precision or complex explanation requires it; use an outline when natural narration is better. For every ATLAS development episode, prepare a voiceover that explains the actual work performed during that session.

## 6. Record voiceover
Record a short test first. Use a quiet environment where practical, keep a consistent microphone distance, and speak naturally. Re-record unclear sections rather than trying to repair everything in editing.

## 7. Edit
Remove dead time and repetition. Add readable labels, zooms, diagrams, or cutaways only when they help. Balance narration and music. Keep important code and interface details visible long enough to understand.

## 8. Quality check
Review the complete export. Check audio, captions, spelling, links, privacy, technical accuracy, pacing, and export integrity. Verify that the title and thumbnail honestly represent the technical subject. Ensure that any game footage is clearly serving a technical demonstration rather than promising gameplay.

## 9. Publish
Write a clear title and description, choose a relevant thumbnail, add the correct technical playlist, and review platform settings. Publish when the video is ready, not because an arbitrary deadline says it must be.

## 10. Learn
Review useful audience feedback and analytics when available. Record one or two improvements for the next video. Avoid making large strategy changes based on one upload.

This is a flexible checklist, not mandatory bureaucracy. Skip steps that genuinely do not apply. The workflow should make creating easier, not become a second project.
